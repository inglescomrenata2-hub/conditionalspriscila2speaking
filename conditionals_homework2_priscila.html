Part 1 — Choose the best option

Choose the option that best completes each sentence. Pay attention to meaning and register.

1. If the contract ___ governed by Californian law, the non-compete clause would be unenforceable.
2. You can submit the brief late ___ you get written permission from the professor.
3. If I had read the fine print, I ___ that lease.
4. If water ___ 100°C, it boils — and if a deadline falls on a weekend, it ___ to the next business day.
5. Keep a copy of every email to the client; ___, you'll have no record if a dispute arises.
6. ___ for her quick thinking, the deal would have collapsed during the final negotiation.
1. were  |  2. provided that  |  3. wouldn't have signed  |  4. reaches / moves  |  5. otherwise  |  6. Had it not been

Note: in 6, "If it hadn't been for…" is also correct — "Had it not been for…" is the formal, inverted version.

Part 2 — Rewrite the sentences

Rewrite each situation as one conditional sentence. Choose the conditional that fits the meaning.

1. I didn't check the statute of limitations. We missed the deadline.

2. I don't speak Spanish fluently, so I can't take on clients from the Latino community on my own.

3. The opposing party might accept our offer. Then we'll avoid a costly trial.

4. The firm didn't offer remote work, so two associates resigned.

5. You sign a document without reading it. You take a huge risk. (general truth)

6. My professor doesn't give extensions. I have to submit the paper on Friday.

Suggested answers:
1. If I had checked the statute of limitations, we wouldn't have missed the deadline. (third)
2. If I spoke Spanish fluently, I could take on clients from the Latino community on my own. (second)
3. If the opposing party accepts our offer, we'll avoid a costly trial. (first)
4. If the firm had offered remote work, two associates wouldn't have resigned. (third)
5. If you sign a document without reading it, you take a huge risk. (zero)
6. If my professor gave extensions, I wouldn't have to submit the paper on Friday. (second)

Part 3 — Find and correct the mistake

Each sentence has ONE mistake. Rewrite it correctly.

1. "If I would have known about the hearing, I would have prepared better."

2. "If the client will call, please tell her I'm in court."

3. "If I was a partner at this firm, I would change the billing policy." (formal register)

4. "Unless you don't submit the form, your application won't be processed."

5. "Hadn't the judge intervened, the argument would have escalated."

6. "If she had passed the bar exam last year, she would work at a big firm last summer."

1. If I had known about the hearing… (never "would" in the if-clause)
2. If the client calls, please tell her I'm in court.
3. If I were a partner at this firm… ("was" is common in speech, but "were" is expected in formal English)
4. Unless you submit the form… (unless = if… not — no double negative)
5. Had the judge not intervened, the argument would have escalated.
6. …she would have worked at a big firm last summer. (both halves are in the past)

Part 4 — Writing

4a. A client asks you by email: "What happens if I just ignore the lawsuit?" Write a short, professional reply (80–100 words) explaining the possible consequences. Use at least one zero, one first and one formal inversion (Should…).

Model answer (one possibility):
Dear Mr. Thompson, thank you for your message. I strongly advise against ignoring the lawsuit. In California, if a defendant doesn't respond within 30 days, the plaintiff can request a default judgment. If that happens, the court will likely award the full amount claimed, and it may be very difficult to reverse. Should you decide to respond, we will review the complaint together and file an answer well before the deadline. Please let me know by Friday how you would like to proceed. Best regards, [Name]

4b. Think about your journey from practising law in Brazil to working and studying in the US. Write a paragraph (120–150 words) about it using all four conditionals: a third (a decision in the past), a second (an imagined present), a first (a plan for the future) and a zero (a general truth you've learned).
